# Prototype yard layout subsystem

Reviewed: 2026-09-24, implementation introduced alongside this spec.

`scripts/level/yard_layout.gd` is a RefCounted data factory. `create_layout(variant: int = 0) -> Dictionary` returns fresh caller-owned data; variants wrap modulo two. World bounds are `Rect2(0, 0, 1200, 480)`, ground top is 440, crane anchor is `(200, 45)`, and pickup bounds are `Rect2(85, 350, 350, 90)`.

`scrap` contains ten dictionaries (five steel, three copper, two rubber) with unique integer `id`, StringName `material` (`copper`, `rubber`, `steel`), enlarged PNG `texture` path, center `position`, bounding-rectangle `size`, and positive `mass`. They are stacked in rows of 4, 3, 2 and 1 around x285 with 8-unit gaps and a fixed jitter under half the gap, so no two start overlapping and physics tumbles them into a heap. `tool_stand` is the first head stand's centre; the salvage round places a second 80 units to its right. `bins` contains three dictionaries with material, center position, bounding size and texture path; they are centered at x650/792/934, y390, size150×100, spaced `150 - SortingBin.WALL` so neighbours share a wall. The earlier 50-unit gaps trapped scrap narrower than the magnet could reach into (found 2026-09-25). The second variant rearranges scrap and bin categories. No shared mutable state or random generator is involved.

These dimensions, masses and material assignments are **diagnostic prototype fixtures**, not approved gameplay objects or physically inferred art bounds. The caller decides whether to build any collision, sensor or gameplay object from the data. Texture transparency is not converted into geometry. No physics body, scoring, timer, magnet or scene transition is owned here.

`scripts/level/yard_backdrop.gd` is a Node2D presentation helper; `configure(layout)` copies layout data and redraws the yard with linear sampling of existing 8× art: a sky fill matching the top of the fence tile, the scrapyard fence tiled along the floor, lattice towers at both ends under the rail (the cap and foot drawn once, one 4-pixel lattice period repeated between), then the floor and rail. When present, `backdrop_skyline_tile` stands on the fence tile's opaque top sky rows (`FENCE_SKY_ROWS` 10) and `backdrop_junk_heap` sits dimmed in the right-hand corner, clear of the pickup area and bins so it cannot be mistaken for scrap. `draw_sky = false` lets a caller layer its own sky behind. `scripts/level/yard_ambience.gd` is that sky for the salvage round: a `far` layer (z −11) with a banded night gradient ending in the fence's sky colour, 70 seeded twinkling stars, a glowing moon, drifting clouds and occasional gull fly-bys, and a `near` layer (z −4) with two floodlights casting flickering light cones, blinking beacons on the tower tops, a crow hopping along the fence rail, a rat crossing the floor now and then and smoke rising from the heap. It runs with `PROCESS_MODE_ALWAYS` so the yard stays alive on the start, pause and results screens, supplies no collision, and skips any sprite that is absent. Layouts carry `art_scale` (1.6 world units per source pixel), which sizes the fence, towers and the salvage lab's magnet, trolley and effects. It supplies no collision. Callers place their own scrap/bin objects above it. `labs/level/main.tscn` demonstrates the data without physics, preserves each scrap sprite's aspect ratio within its diagnostic rectangle, fits the stage to the window, and switches variants through a button or Tab. It does not alter the main scene.

`tests/level_test.gd` verifies deterministic independent data, spawn separation and bounds, positive masses, unique IDs, represented materials, bin separation and floor alignment, resource availability, and instantiated lab switching. Run through the shared Godot Podman wrapper after editor import. These checks establish no reachability or fun claim; integration playtests must evaluate pickup reliability, lift clearance, material recognition and drop accuracy.
